TIER 2 · ROOTS

Air

We breathe around 10,000 liters of air a day, most of it indoors. Lungs, a bird’s air sacs, a cat’s nose and a houseplant’s leaves all need the same thing: air that moves and isn’t too wet. Four units: two foundations, then the two ways things get into the air, with the fastest fix for each.

What’s in the room right now?
Foundations 1 · Airflow

Air needs to move: in fresh, out stale. Windows, fans and filters are the three tools, and almost every later fix leans on them.

 2 · Damp

Water vapor is the other half. Between 30 and 50% humidity, mold and dust mites don’t settle in, and wood, lungs and skin are comfortable.
How things get into the air 3 · Heat · short-lived

Cooking, flames, an overheated pan, a candle. Heat sets particles and fumes loose all at once. They’re strong but brief, and airflow clears them in minutes.

 4 · Dust · long-lasting

Furniture, walls, floors and the ground release things slowly, or only when disturbed. They settle into dust and stay for years. Vacuuming and wet cleaning carry them out.

Short-lived things answer to airflow. Long-lasting things answer to cleaning and to leaving well-sealed materials alone.

Fun fact

A single gas burner on high, or a pan of something searing, can push fine particles in a kitchen higher than a smoggy day outside, and an open window plus a fan pointing out can bring them back down in well under an hour. Air answers fast, which makes it one of the most satisfying needs to meet.

Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, Indoor Environment Group, residential cooking exposure studies (Singer et al., 2017; Logue et al., 2014). Laws mentioned are California’s; the physics is the same everywhere.

    Airflow

    Air needs to move

    1.1

    Stale air you can measure

    Every breath out adds carbon dioxide (CO₂). Outdoors it sits around 420 parts per million (ppm). In a closed bedroom with two sleepers it can pass 2,000 by morning. CO₂ itself is mostly a messenger: when it’s high, everything else people and the house release is building up too, so it’s the easiest single gauge of airflow.

    Around 400–800 Fresh

    Close to outdoor air. Plenty of air is moving through.

    800–1,200 Getting stale

    Fine for a while. A good moment to crack a window.

    Over 1,200 Stuffy

    Often felt as heavy, sleepy or headachy air. Studies link levels like this with slower thinking and lighter sleep.

    The first breath back into a bedroom after a walk is the free meter: if it smells like “people”, the air hasn’t been changing.

    How to fixRight nowSet up onceFree → $
    1. Free · todaySleep with a window open a crack, or the bedroom door open, even an inch.
    2. FreeUse your nose: step outside for a minute, then walk back in.
    3. $ · onceA CO₂ meter with an “NDIR” sensor (about $30–$80) makes airflow visible. Libraries in some cities lend them; one meter can travel room to room or house to house.

    ASHRAE position document on indoor carbon dioxide (2022); Allen et al., Environmental Health Perspectives 2016 (COGfx study); Strøm-Tejsen et al., Indoor Air 2016 (bedroom CO₂ and sleep).

    1.2

    Windows and cross-breeze

    One open window lets a little air trade places. Two openings on different sides make a path, and air moves through many times faster. Height helps too: warm air rises and leaves through a high opening while cooler air comes in low (the stack effect).

    • A 5 to 10 minute wide-open “purge” swaps most of a room’s air, even in winter, and walls and furniture hold their warmth.
    • A fan in a window pointing out works better than one pointing in: it pulls from the whole room, and fresh air finds its own way through every other gap.
    • Interior doors are part of the path. Open them between the two windows.
    How to fixRight nowHabitFree
    1. Free · todayFind your best cross-breeze pair: two windows on different sides, with open doors between.
    2. FreeA morning purge: both wide open for 5–10 minutes while the kettle boils. Kids love being the window crew.
    3. FreeAny fan you own, set in the far window facing out.
    Vehicle or boatSmall spaces fill up fastFor you

    A van or cabin has a fraction of a room’s air, so breath and cooking build up in minutes. A roof vent fan pulling out plus one window cracked on the far side is the classic setup. Never run a fuel heater, stove or engine for warmth in a closed vehicle; carbon monoxide builds silently (3.3).

    Can’t change muchAirflow with nothing permanentFor you

    Everything here is portable: windows, doors, a box fan, a filter. A window fan that sits in the frame with no screws comes along to the next place.

    US EPA, Guide to Air Cleaners in the Home and ventilation guidance; Building Science Corporation on the stack effect.

    1.3

    Fans that pull air out

    A window lets air drift. An exhaust fan grabs it where it’s made: steam in the bathroom, smoke over the stove. The catch is the duct: a fan only removes air if it leads outside.

    FanWhat it removesMake it count
    Range hood, vented outsideSmoke, grease, gas combustion fumes, steamTurn it on before the burner, use the back burners, leave it on 10–15 minutes after.
    Range hood, recirculatingGrease (on its filter); some odor with a carbon filterIt doesn’t remove fumes, steam or fine particles. Pair it with a window.
    Bathroom fanSteam, odorsRun during the shower and about 20 minutes after. Hold a tissue to it: if it doesn’t cling, the fan or duct needs cleaning.
    Ceiling or standing fanNothing; it stirsComfortable, but it moves air around, not out. Point it at a window to help.

    Green: removes it · Amber: partly · Red: moves it around.

    2. FreeHood on before the burner; bathroom fan on before the shower.
    3. Free → $Soak a greasy hood filter in hot water with baking soda and a drop of dish soap; dust off a bathroom fan grille.
    No vented hoodGetting kitchen air out anywayFor you
    • A box fan in the nearest window facing out, running while you cook, does much of a hood’s job.
    • Lids on pots cut steam and spatter at the source.

    1.4

    Filters that clean moving air

    A filter catches particles (smoke, dust, pollen, mold spores, the tiny droplets that carry colds) from air that’s pushed through it. It doesn’t remove gases like CO₂, cooking fumes or most smells; that’s what airflow is for. Three labels tell you what you’re getting:

    HEPA Purifiers

    Catches at least 99.97% of particles at the hardest size to catch. Real HEPA, not “HEPA-type”.

    MERV 13 Furnace & box fans

    Catches most fine smoke particles. A good balance of clean and easy airflow.

    CADR How much

    Clean air delivery rate: how many cubic feet of clean air per minute. Bigger room, bigger number.

    The box-fan filter. A 20-inch box fan with a MERV 13 filter strapped to the back is a well-tested, low-cost purifier. Four or five filters taped into a cube around the fan (a Corsi–Rosenthal box) clean several times more air, often rivaling purifiers that cost far more. Neighbors build them together on a Saturday and share leftover filters.

    Skip ozone generators, “activated oxygen” and most ionizers. Ozone irritates lungs and reacts with scents to make formaldehyde. Houseplants are lovely kin, but a home would need hundreds per room to clean air measurably.

    Air cleaners sold in California have to meet CARB’s ozone limit (0.050 ppm). CARB keeps a list of certified devices.

    How to fixSet up onceTogether$
    1. Free · firstAirflow first. A filter is for particles you can’t open a window on: smoke days, a busy road, a cold.
    2. $ · onceBox fan + MERV 13 filter, or a four-filter cube. Size it with the tool below. Use a newer fan (2012 or later) with a UL or ETL mark.
    3. TogetherBuild a batch with neighbors, a school or a library, and keep a few ready for fire season.

    US EPA, Guide to Air Cleaners in the Home and EPA research on DIY box-fan air cleaners; Dal Porto et al., Aerosol and Air Quality Research 2022 (Corsi–Rosenthal box); Cummings & Waring, Journal of Exposure Science & Environmental Epidemiology 2019 (plants); CARB Air Cleaner Regulation (2010).

    1.5

    When outside air is worse

    Most days, outside air is cleaner than inside. Wildfire smoke, a freeway at rush hour and high pollen days flip that. Then the plan turns around: close up, keep the air moving through a filter, and make one room the clean room.

    Air Quality IndexMeansIndoors
    0–100 · Good to moderateOpen up as usualWindows, cross-breeze, purge.
    101–150 · Sensitive groupsKids, elders, pregnancy, asthma, companion animals feel itClose the windows on that side, run a filter in the bedroom.
    151+ · UnhealthyEveryoneClean room: windows shut, filter on high, no frying or candles, fans recirculating only.

    AirNow.gov and the Fire and Smoke Map show smoke in real time. Many counties open cleaner-air centers on smoke days.

    Birds, cats with asthma, older dogs and outdoor neighbors like hens all feel smoke early. Bring companions inside the clean room; leave water out for wild visitors, whose need for water rises on smoky days.

    How to fixSet up onceTogetherFree → $
    1. Free · nowChoose the clean room: usually the bedroom with the fewest windows. Save AirNow on your phone.
    2. $One filter ready for that room, with a spare filter, before fire season.
    3. TogetherCheck on neighbors who live alone or have no filter; lend yours if their room needs it more.
    Near a freewayThe busy-road rhythmFor you

    Traffic pollution is highest within about 500 feet and peaks at rush hours. Air out on the side away from the road, in the middle of the day or late evening, and run a filter in the bedroom overnight.

    Near fieldsSpray days and dustFor you

    Unit 2 · Foundation

    Damp

    Water in the air, and where it lands

    Air always carries some water you can’t see. Warm air holds more; cool air holds less, so when warm, damp air meets a cold window or wall, the water lands. Where it lands and stays, mold and dust mites move in. Keep it in the middle range and most of that never starts.

    Habit Lids, fans and drying habits do most of it. Set up once A $10 humidity meter shows where you are.

    2.1

    The humidity sweet spot

    Relative humidity (RH) is how full the air is of water, as a share of the most it could hold at that temperature. A humidity meter (hygrometer) costs about $10 and is one of the most useful things a home can own.

    Under 30%Dry skin, static, sore throats, cracking wood
    30–50%Sweet spot
    50–60%Dust mites thrive
    Over 60%Mold, musty smells, condensation, peeling paint
    0305060100%

    1. $ · onceOne hygrometer (about $10). Check it in the morning, after showers and after cooking for a week.
    2. FreeMove it room to room: the bedroom, the bathroom, the closet on an outside wall.
    3. FreeOver 60% for days at a time? Unit 2.2 has the sources; 2.3 has the cold spots.

    US EPA, A Brief Guide to Mold, Moisture and Your Home; Arlian et al. on the humidity dust mites need.

    2.2

    Where the water comes from

    A household adds several liters of water to the air every day without anyone spilling a drop. Most of it comes from a handful of places, and each has a free fix.

    SourceThe fix
    Showers and bathsFan on, door shut while it runs, window cracked. Squeegee the walls: it takes a minute and the water goes down the drain, not into the air.
    Cooking, especially boilingLids on. Hood or window fan on.
    Laundry drying indoorsOutside when you can; inside, one room with the door shut and the window open.
    Breathing and sleepingA cracked window at night (1.1).
    Leaks, wet ground, crawlspacesFind and stop. Gutters and soil sloping away from the walls keep rain off them.
    Plants, aquariums, unvented dryers and gas heatersLids on tanks; dryers vented outside; unvented gas heaters add both water and fumes (3.3).
    How to fixHabitFree
    1. Free · todayLids on boiling pots and a 1-minute shower squeegee: two habits kids can own.
    2. FreePick a drying room for laundry, door shut, window open.
    3. FreeWalk the outside after rain: where does water pool against the house?

    Building Science Corporation, moisture sources in houses; Lstiburek, Moisture Control Handbook.

    2.3

    Condensation and cold spots

    Condensation is the same as a cold glass sweating. Water lands on the coldest surface the damp air can reach: single-pane windows, outside corners, the wall behind a dresser where warm air never gets in. Morning fog on the windows is the house showing you its cold spots.

    • Wipe it, don’t leave it. A dedicated towel for the windows each morning. Water left on the sill feeds mold on the frame.
    • Let warm air reach the walls. Furniture a few inches out from outside walls; closet doors cracked; beds not jammed into corners.
    • Even heat helps. A room left very cold next to warm ones collects the house’s water.
    How to fixHabitFree
    1. Free · todayPull furniture off outside walls by 2–4 inches.
    2. FreeMorning window wipe, then a 5-minute purge (1.2) to carry the water out.
    3. FreeLook behind the bed and in closets on outside walls for gray spots or a musty smell.
    Vehicle or boatMetal walls and morning dripsFor you

    Thin metal walls are always cold, so condensation comes every night. A roof fan on low while sleeping, a cracked window and a small towel routine make the most difference. Wool and cork stay comfortable when damp; check under the mattress for moisture weekly.

    US EPA, Mold Remediation in Schools and Commercial Buildings (condensation and dew point); Building Science Corporation.

    2.4

    Where mold starts

    Mold spores float in every breath of outdoor air. Indoors, they only grow where something stays wet for a day or two. So mold is a message about water: it shows exactly where the house needs drying. Damp homes are linked with more coughing, wheezing and asthma, in children most.

    • A musty or earthy smell is mold you can’t see yet.
    • Gray, black or green specks along window frames, grout, ceilings over showers and the back of closets.
    • Soft or stained drywall and swollen wood point to a hidden leak.
    How to fixHabitTogetherFree → $
    1. Free · firstFind and stop the water. Mold comes back as long as the water does.
    2. FreeSmall patches (under about 10 square feet): scrub with soap and water, then dry fully with a fan. Bleach isn’t needed and adds fumes.
    3. AskLarger areas, a hidden leak or anything after a flood: the owner, or a professional, ideally with the source fixed first.
    RentingWhen the leak isn’t yours to fixFor you

    Since 2016, visible mold that affects health counts as a substandard-housing condition in California (SB 655). Write to the landlord with photos and dates and keep a copy; local code enforcement can inspect.

    Toxins → Living toxins (mold on food, what molds make) · Cleaning lens (mold on surfaces)

    WHO, Guidelines for Indoor Air Quality: Dampness and Mould (2009); US EPA, A Brief Guide to Mold, Moisture and Your Home; California SB 655 (2015).

    2.5

    Too dry

    Winter heating and desert air can drop humidity below 30%. Skin cracks, noses bleed, throats get scratchy, wood floors gap and static zaps fly. The fix is gentle: a little water back in, not a lot.

    • Air-dry a rack of laundry indoors; leave bathwater in the tub until it cools.
    • A pot of water simmering on the stove (lid off, hood off) for a while.
    • A humidifier, if you use one: clean it every few days and fill it with distilled water, or it can spread mold and a fine white mineral dust. Stop at 50%.

    Many houseplants are from humid forests. Grouping them together on a tray of pebbles and water raises the air around their leaves without raising the room.

    How to fixHabitFree
    1. FreeCheck the meter before adding water: dry air feels different from cold air.
    2. FreeLaundry rack inside on dry days; outside on damp ones.
    3. FreeGroup houseplants on a pebble tray.

    US EPA, Indoor Air Facts: Use and Care of Home Humidifiers; US CPSC humidifier safety alert.

    Unit 3 · Short-lived

    Heat

    Fumes and particles that come and go

    Heat is the fastest way things get into air. A searing pan, a gas flame, a pan left empty on high, a candle: each releases a burst of particles and gases at once. The burst is strong but brief. With the air moving, most of it is gone within the hour, so the fixes here are about the moment: lower heat, fan on, window open.

    3.1

    Heat sets things loose

    Heat does three things to the air. It burns (smoke and soot from food, wax, wood, gas). It breaks down materials that are stable when cool (an overheated nonstick coating, the first run of a dusty space heater). And it speeds up what materials slowly breathe out anyway: a new couch in a hot room or a car in the sun gives off its fumes several times faster.

    Burns Particles

    Searing, toasting, frying, candles, incense, wood fires, gas flames.

    Breaks down Fumes

    Nonstick coatings over about 500°F, dust on heater coils, plastic left on a hot surface.

    Speeds up Off-gassing

    New furniture, paint, a hot car interior. Unit 4.3 covers the slow version.

    What makes heat exposures short-lived is that they stop when the heat stops. The level peaks while cooking or burning, then falls as air carries it out. How fast it falls is up to airflow: a sealed kitchen can stay smoky for hours; a kitchen with a vented hood or a window fan clears in minutes.

    How to fixHabitFree
    1. Free · todayAir on before heat: hood, window fan or cross-breeze, then the burner.
    2. FreeRun a new space heater or the first heat of the season with the window open; the burnt-dust smell passes in an hour or so.
    3. FreePark in shade and open the car windows for a minute before driving on hot days.

    US EPA, Introduction to Indoor Air Quality; Parthasarathy et al., Journal of the Air & Waste Management Association 2011 (temperature, humidity and formaldehyde emissions).

    3.2

    Cooking smoke

    Cooking is the biggest indoor source of fine particles in most homes, gas or electric. High heat and oil make the most: searing, stir-frying, broiling, toasting. Boiling and steaming make very little. Burnt food makes a lot.

    CookingParticlesEasy shift
    Boiling, steaming, simmeringLowLid on for the steam.
    Baking, roastingMedium, more when it drips or burnsA tray under drips; oven light instead of opening the door.
    Frying, searing, broiling, toastingHighMedium heat, oils with a higher smoke point, back burner, hood on high.

    Back burners matter: most range hoods catch far more from the back than the front. Switching a pan back is free and makes a measurable difference.

    Kids next to the stove are learning to cook. A step stool at the back-burner side, a lid in their hands and the hood switch as their job makes them part of the clean air.

    How to fixHabitFree
    1. Free · todayBack burners first, hood on high or a window fan out.
    2. FreeMedium heat for oil; if it smokes, pull the pan off and let it cool.
    3. FreeLeave the fan running 10–15 minutes after the burner’s off.
    No vented hoodClearing smoke with a windowFor you

    A box fan in the nearest window facing out, plus another window open somewhere else in the home, removes much of what a hood would. For high-heat cooking, a camping stove or electric pan on a porch or balcony keeps the smoke outside entirely.

    Food → Cook (safe heat, little fuel)

    Abdullahi et al., Atmospheric Environment 2013 (cooking particle emissions review); Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, range hood studies (Delp & Singer 2012, back vs front burners).

    3.3

    Gas flames

    A gas flame burns methane, and burning makes more than heat. It makes nitrogen dioxide (NO₂), which inflames airways; a little carbon monoxide (CO); and, along with leaked unburned gas, small amounts of benzene and formaldehyde. All of this happens whether or not food is on the burner.

    • An estimated 1 in 8 cases of childhood asthma in the US is linked to gas stove use, about the same share as secondhand smoke.
    • In small kitchens without a vented hood, NO₂ from a stove can pass the outdoor health limit within an hour of cooking.
    • Unvented gas wall heaters, oven doors left open for warmth and fuel heaters in vans release the same gases straight into the room.

    Carbon monoxide has no smell. Headache, dizziness, nausea or sleepiness in more than one person (or companion animal) at once means fresh air first: everyone outside, then call 911. A rotten-egg smell is a gas leak: no switches or flames; leave and call from outside.

    Homes with a gas appliance, fireplace or attached garage need a carbon monoxide alarm (Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Prevention Act, 2010).

    How to fixRight nowSet up onceFree → $
    1. Free · nowHood or window fan every time a burner is on; not using the oven or stovetop for heat.
    2. $ · onceA CO alarm near sleeping areas (about $20–$40); test it monthly.
    3. $An induction hot plate (about $60–$100) and an electric kettle move most daily cooking off the flame, and come with you when you move.
    Gas stoveLiving well with the stove you haveFor you
    • Fan on before the burner, back burners first, smallest burner that does the job.
    • A blue, steady flame burns cleaner than a yellow, flickering one; a yellow flame is worth a call to the gas utility, which often checks for free.
    • Kids, pregnancy, asthma and birds: these are the times an induction plate for daily cooking matters most.
    RentingAsking about the stoveFor you

    Some California cities and utilities offer induction rebates and loaner programs for renters and owners alike. A request framed around a shared need (cleaner air for a child with asthma, lower bills) often goes well.

    Emotions & love → Requests · Governance (shared air)

    3.4

    Overheated nonstick

    Most nonstick coatings are PTFE (often sold as Teflon), a PFAS plastic. At normal cooking heat it stays put. Above about 500°F (260°C) it starts to break down, and above about 660°F it releases a mix of fluorinated gases and very fine particles, some of them PFAS themselves. An empty nonstick pan on high can pass 500°F in a few minutes.

    People Air

    Breathing the fumes can cause “polymer fume fever”: chills, aches and a fever for a day or two, often mistaken for flu.

    Birds Air

    Birds breathe so efficiently that these fumes can kill a bird within minutes, even from another room.

    Short-lived Gone fast

    The fumes clear with airflow, like any heat burst. The bigger long-term question is the pan itself; Toxins 3.4 covers the swap.

    A sharp, chemical or “hot plastic” smell from a pan: heat off, pan to a cold burner, windows open, fan out, and birds and people to the freshest room.

    How to fixHabitSet up onceFree → $
    1. Free · todayNonstick on low to medium only, never preheated empty, always with the fan on.
    2. FreeSearing, broiling and anything on high goes in cast iron or steel.
    3. $ · as they wearWhen a nonstick pan scratches or flakes, let it go and replace it with cast iron, carbon steel or stainless (secondhand ones last a lifetime).
    BirdsKeeping your bird safe from kitchen fumesFor you

    3.5

    Candles, incense and scent

    Flames and smolder make particles: incense most of all, then paraffin candles (the black soot on the jar), then wood fires. Fragrance adds a second layer: many scent chemicals react with ozone in the air to make formaldehyde and new ultrafine particles, so plug-in fresheners and diffusers add to the air even with no flame.

    • Incense burned in a closed room can make more fine particles than a busy road.
    • Beeswax or soy candles with short-trimmed cotton wicks smoke less than paraffin; a draft makes any candle sooty.
    • Wood stoves and fireplaces leak smoke into the room each time the door opens or the draft reverses.

    Cats can’t break down many essential oils well, and birds react to scented air quickly. Diffusers belong in rooms companions can leave, if anywhere.

    How to fixHabitFree
    1. Free · todayUnplug any plug-in fresheners; open a window for smell instead (it removes the cause rather than covering it).
    2. FreeCandles and incense with a window open, for a shorter time, or outside on a porch.
    3. FreeSomeone in the house smokes or vapes? Outside, away from open windows, is the strategy that meets everyone’s needs.

    Toxins → In the house → Fragrance

    Unit 4 · Long-lasting

    Dust

    Slow sources, and what settles and stays

    Some things reach the air slowly: a couch breathing out, paint wearing off a window frame, a gas seeping up from the ground. What they release settles into dust, and indoors, out of sun and rain, dust keeps it for years. Airflow helps a little here. Cleaning helps a lot, and so does leaving well-sealed old materials alone.

    4.1

    Dust is a sponge

    House dust is skin, hair, fibers, pollen and soil, and it soaks up chemicals from everything around it: flame retardants from foam, phthalates from vinyl, PFAS from treated fabrics, lead from old paint, pesticides tracked in on shoes. Outdoors, sun and rain break many of these down in days. Indoors they can last for years.

    Floors Where it lands

    Most dust ends on the floor and in rugs, right where crawling babies, toddlers and companion animals spend their time.

    Hands How it moves

    Little ones swallow an estimated few dozen milligrams of dust and soil a day, hand to mouth.

    Air When stirred

    Walking, sweeping and dry dusting toss the finest dust back into the air for an hour or more.

    How to fixHabitFree
    1. Free · todayShoes off at the door and a mat outside and in. It cuts tracked-in lead and pesticides more than any other single step.
    2. FreeWash hands before eating, especially kids after floor and yard play.
    3. FreeDamp cloth instead of a feather duster: it carries dust out rather than lifting it.

    4.2

    Vacuuming that helps

    Vacuuming is the main way long-lasting things leave a home. The vacuum matters, though: one without a good filter catches the big bits and blows the finest dust, the part that carries the most chemicals, right back out the exhaust.

    • A HEPA filter and a sealed body. “HEPA” on the label means little if air leaks around the filter; “sealed system” is the phrase to look for. Bagged vacuums keep dust in when emptied.
    • Slow passes. Slow strokes, several over the same spot, lift far more from carpet than quick ones.
    • Window open, then back. Vacuuming lifts some fine dust for a while. A window open and little ones and companions in another room for half an hour gives it time to settle or leave.
    • Then a damp mop. On hard floors, a damp microfiber mop after vacuuming catches what the vacuum missed. Dry sweeping mostly stirs it.
    • Empty it outside and wash or tap out filters outdoors.

    A weekly “dust round”: one person vacuums, one damp-wipes sills and baseboards, the smallest one wrings out cloths. Music on, ten minutes, done together.

    How to fixHabitTogetherFree → $$
    1. Free · this weekVacuum slowly with a window open, then damp-mop hard floors. Once a week (twice with crawlers or companions shedding).
    2. FreeBorrow a HEPA vacuum from a tool library or neighbor before buying one.
    3. $$ · onceIf buying: sealed HEPA, bagged if possible. Secondhand ones with a fresh filter work well.
    Little onesFloors for crawlersFor you

    A washable cotton rug or play mat that goes in the laundry weekly gives a clean zone on the floor. Wet-wipe window sills too: in older homes they’re where lead dust collects (4.4).

    CompanionsFur, dander and noses near the floorFor you

Washable bedding, a brushing routine outside, and vacuuming their favorite spots help them and anyone with allergies.     4.3

    New things breathe out

    New furniture, mattresses, flooring, carpet and paint release chemicals (VOCs, and formaldehyde from pressed wood) for weeks to months. The “new smell” is the strongest part; it fades as the material settles, and faster when the space is warm and well aired. Things that are already old have mostly finished.

    • Secondhand is already aired out. Used furniture and solid-wood pieces have done most of their off-gassing years ago.
    • Air new things out first in a garage, porch or spare room with the window open, for a week or two if you can.
    • Low- or zero-VOC paint, windows open and a fan out while it dries, and a few days before sleeping in the room.

    Composite wood sold in California has had to meet CARB’s formaldehyde limits since 2009 (now national). Upholstered furniture carries a label saying whether it contains added flame retardants (SB 1019); “contains NO added flame retardants” is the one to look for.

    How to fixHabitFree
    1. Free · firstLook secondhand, swap or borrow before buying new.
    2. FreeNew item? A week or two airing out somewhere other than a bedroom.
    3. FreePainting or new floors: windows open and a fan out for several days afterward.

    4.4

    Asbestos and lead: only when disturbed

    Two older materials stay harmless while they’re whole and sealed, and only reach the air when they’re damaged, sanded, drilled, scraped or crumbling. That’s the whole key: intact, leave it; disturbed, take care.

    MaterialWhere it may beHow it gets out
    Asbestos (homes built before about 1980)Popcorn or textured ceilings, vinyl floor tiles and their glue, pipe and boiler wrap, vermiculite attic insulation, cement siding, old duct tapeFibers break loose when it’s cut, sanded, drilled, scraped or crumbling. Tiny and light, they float for hours and lodge in lungs for life.
    Lead paint (homes built before 1978)Window frames and sills, doors, stairs, porches, trimRubbing and chipping turn paint to dust. Windows opening and closing grind it fine. Dry sanding or scraping releases a lot at once.

    Suspect crumbling asbestos? Keep people and animals away from it, don’t sweep or vacuum it (an ordinary vacuum spreads the fibers), mist it lightly with water and cover it, and get it tested.

    Asbestos work over 100 square feet has to be done by a contractor registered with Cal/OSHA. Contractors working on pre-1978 paint in homes need EPA lead-safe (RRP) certification.

    4.5

    From the ground: radon

    Radon is a natural radioactive gas from uranium in rock and soil. It seeps up through cracks and gaps in a foundation and collects in the lowest floors. It has no smell or color, and over many years it’s the second leading cause of lung cancer after smoking (the first among people who’ve never smoked). It’s the clearest case of a slow, long-lasting source: steady, every day, for as long as you live there.

    • Only testing tells. Neighboring houses can differ a lot.
    • A short-term test kit costs about $15–$30. The US action level is 4 picocuries per liter (pCi/L).
    • Above that, a sealed-crack and small-fan system under the floor usually brings it down by more than half.
    • Apartments above the second floor are rarely affected.

    The California Geological Survey maps areas with higher radon potential; parts of the Sierra foothills, Santa Barbara and Ventura counties and others are higher. The California Department of Public Health has low-cost test kits.

    How to fixSet up once$
    1. $ · onceOne radon test on the lowest lived-in floor, in fall or winter with windows mostly shut.
    2. FreeUnder 4 pCi/L: done. Seal visible foundation cracks anyway.
    3. AskOver 4: a certified mitigator. Owners on board? It’s a one-time fix that lasts.
